How much do AI agents cost for a business? What actually sets the price

6 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Pranav Arya

It is the first question everyone asks, and the honest answer is "it depends". That sounds like a dodge, so here is what it depends on, and how to work out a sensible budget before you talk to anyone.

We are deliberately not quoting a price list here. Numbers you find online vary wildly, and many are written to sell something. What matters is knowing what drives the cost, so you can judge any quote you receive.

The five things that set the price

1. How complex the job is. An agent that summarizes one weekly report is a small build. An agent that researches, drafts, checks and files results across several tools is a bigger one. The more steps and the more decisions, the more design and testing it takes.

2. How many tools it connects to. Email, spreadsheets, a CRM, a shared drive, WhatsApp. Every connection has to be set up, secured and tested. Two tools is a very different job from ten.

3. How much checking it needs. If a mistake would be cheap and obvious, light review is enough. If errors could be costly, you need stronger checks and more human approval steps, which means more work to build well.

4. How much it runs. AI models charge for what they process. An agent that handles ten tasks a week costs very little to run. One that handles thousands a day is a real line in your budget.

5. Who looks after it afterwards. Agents are not install-and-forget. Websites change, tools update, your business changes. Someone has to watch the logs and adjust the agent as things shift.

Two kinds of cost to separate

  • Build cost: designing, setting up and testing the agent. Usually a one-off, or the first phase of a project.
  • Running cost: the AI usage, hosting, and the ongoing care. This recurs, and it is the number people forget to ask about.

When you get a quote, ask for both, written down separately.

How to judge whether it is worth it

Start with the other side of the sum. How many hours a week does this task take today, and what is an hour of that person's time worth to you? If a task eats ten hours a week and an agent can handle most of it, the saving is easy to see. If you cannot name the hours, you are not ready to buy yet, and that is a fine thing to find out early.

A simple rule: start with one small agent on one painful task. Measure the hours saved over a month. Spend more only when the numbers say it is working.

Warning signs in a quote

  • A big price for a "complete AI transformation" with no clear first task.
  • No mention of running costs or who maintains the agent.
  • No logs, no approval steps, no way to see what the agent did.
  • A promise that nobody will need to be involved.

The cheapest way to find out

Run a small pilot. One agent, one task, a few weeks, a clear measure of success. It costs far less than a big project, and it tells you what a wider rollout would really cost, using your own data instead of guesses.
