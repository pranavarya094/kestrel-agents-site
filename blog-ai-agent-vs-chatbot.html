AI agent vs chatbot: what is the difference, and which one does your business need?

3 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Pranav Arya

Ask five vendors what an "AI agent" is and you will get six answers. Half of them are selling a chatbot with a new label. That is not a crime, but it makes buying harder than it needs to be, so here is the difference in plain words.

A chatbot talks. An agent works. That one line covers most of it. The rest is about what follows from it.

What a chatbot does

A chatbot answers questions. You type something, it replies, and the exchange ends there. A good one can answer common customer questions at any hour, point people to the right page, and collect a name and an email address before handing over to a person.

It lives inside a conversation. It does not go off and do things on its own, and it forgets the task the moment the chat is over.

What an agent does

An agent is given a goal and a set of tools, then decides its own next steps. Tell it to "find twenty companies that match this profile and prepare a short note on each" and it will search, read websites, compare what it finds, write the notes and come back when it is done or stuck.

It can use your email, your spreadsheets, your CRM, a browser. It can work for an hour without anyone typing a word. And it can keep a log of what it did, which matters more than it sounds.

A quick way to tell them apart

  • Who starts the work? A chatbot waits for a message. An agent can be handed a task, or a schedule, and get on with it.
  • Where does the work end up? A chatbot produces a reply in a chat window. An agent produces a finished piece of work: a brief, a lead list, a drafted article, an updated record.
  • How many steps? A chatbot handles one step at a time. An agent plans and runs many.

When a chatbot is the right choice

If your problem is repeated questions from customers, such as opening hours, delivery times, pricing or how to book, a chatbot is often enough. It is simpler, cheaper and easier to keep under control. Do not pay for an agent when a good answer box will do.

When you need an agent

Reach for an agent when the work involves several steps, several tools, or a lot of reading and writing. Market research, lead preparation, content production, sorting and replying to an inbox, checking figures across documents. These are jobs where a person currently spends hours moving information from one place to another.

A useful test: if the job could be done by a capable new hire working from a written brief, an agent can probably do it. If all you need is quick answers to common questions, start with a chatbot.

The catch with agents

Because an agent acts on its own, it needs guardrails. It should have access only to what the task requires, it should keep a plain-language record of every step, and a person should approve anything that leaves your company. A chatbot that gives a wrong answer is annoying. An agent that sends a wrong email is a problem, so the checks matter.

You do not have to choose forever

Many businesses end up using both. A chatbot handles the front door and answers the simple questions. Agents work in the background on research, leads, content and operations. Start with whichever one fixes your biggest time drain, and add the other when you have a reason to.
