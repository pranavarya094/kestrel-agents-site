Why every AI agent should keep a log you can read

25 October 2026 · 4 min read · By Pranav Arya

The hardest part of trusting an AI agent is not whether it can do the work. It is knowing what it actually did. If the only thing you see is the final result, you are being asked to trust a black box.

What a good log contains

  • The goal the agent was working towards.
  • What it looked at: pages searched, documents read, records opened.
  • What it decided and, in plain language, why.
  • What it produced and where that output went.
  • Hand-offs to other agents, such as a writer passing a draft to a fact-checker.
  • Approvals: who accepted or rejected each output, and when.

Why it matters

Trust. When you can read the steps, you can judge the work. Fixing problems. When something is wrong, the log shows where it went wrong, so the fix is a change to one step rather than a guess. Accountability. If you work under rules about data or marketing, a record of what was done and who approved it is the first thing a reviewer will ask for. Learning. Reading logs shows where agents waste time, and where your own process has gaps.

What to avoid

Be wary of any system that cannot tell you what it did, that mixes up facts with guesses, or that cites no sources. A confident answer without evidence is not an answer.
